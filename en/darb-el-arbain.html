Darbel-Arba'in.

The Forty Days Road: the time it took a caravan to cross from Darfur to the Nile valley at Asyut. For more than a thousand years it carried trade and culture across the desert.

MeaningRoad of forty days
FromDarfur
ToAsyut, Nile valley
LengthOver 1,700 km
In use1,000+ years
I

A road through the oases

In the Pharaonic and Roman periods the route divided at Kharga and continued through the oases of the Libyan Desert towards the coast.

II

Over 1,700 kilometres

It crossed the Nubian desert and the eastern Sahara. From the south came gold, ivory, gum arabic — and enslaved people. From the north, grain, flax and salt.

III

Roman and Byzantine remains

Wells, fortresses, rest stops with inscriptions and watchtowers lined the road. Some ruins, such as those at Kharga, still stand.

Life inthe caravan.

Caravans of hundreds of camels, led by experienced nomad guides, faced sandstorms, extreme heat and the constant search for water.

Hundreds of camels

Long lines moving at walking pace, a few dozen kilometres a day.

Nomad guides

Navigation by stars, dunes and memory, passed down through generations.

A bridge between worlds

Goods, languages, religions and cultures moved between Africa and Egypt. Pharaohs, Romans and Arab rulers all used and maintained it.

Today the dunes cover much of the route, but it lives on in local memory and in the ruins that mark its path — several of which you will see on our Cairo – Kharga expedition.
