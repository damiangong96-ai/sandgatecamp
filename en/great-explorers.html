Greatexplorers.

Three men who opened routes, drew the first reliable maps and uncovered the secrets of the Egyptian and Libyan Sahara.

I1889 – 1946

Ahmed Hassanein Bey

Egyptian explorer, diplomat, photographer and writer. Born in Cairo and educated at Oxford.

In 1923 he crossed the Libyan Desert from Siwa to Kufra and beyond, mapping parts of the Libyan Sahara that had never been charted. He published his findings in The Geographical Journal and National Geographic.

He later became head of the Egyptian Royal Household and an adviser to King Farouk.

II1874 – 1932

Prince Kamal el Dine Hussein

A member of the Egyptian royal family with a passion for archaeology and desert geography.

Between 1920 and 1924 he financed and led expeditions into the Sahara that produced some of the first accurate maps of the Libyan Desert.

He was a patron of explorers and of the Egyptian Geographical Society.

III1895 – 1951

László Almásy

Hungarian explorer, pilot and adventurer, and the inspiration for The English Patient.

From the 1930s he explored the eastern Sahara by car and by plane, and discovered the Cave of Swimmers in the Gilf Kebir, with its prehistoric paintings.

During the Second World War he served the Axis in North Africa as a pilot and intelligence agent.

Science, adventureand diplomacy.

Between them, these three men opened routes, produced maps and revealed the secrets of the Egyptian and Libyan Sahara. Their legacy is the reason travellers can follow their tracks today.

Several of our routes cross the landscapes they described. Ask us about itineraries inspired by their expeditions.
