Historicoases.

Kharga is the largest of the six great oases of the Western Desert, about 600 km south-west of Cairo. For millennia it was a key stop between the Nile valley and sub-Saharan Africa.

OasisKharga
From Cairo≈ 600 km
TemplesHibis, Ghueita, Zayyan
NecropolisEl-Bagawat
History4,000+ years
I

Desert fortresses

From the Roman period Kharga became a strategic frontier. Military forts were built to protect the trade routes and watch the desert tribes: Qasr el-Labeka, Qasr el-Zayyan and Om el-Dabadib.

II

Temples of the oasis

The Temple of Hibis, dedicated to Amun-Ra, is the only temple of the Persian period in Egypt preserved intact. Romans and Copts later extended it. Nearby stand Qasr el-Ghueita and Qasr el-Zayyan.

III

Necropolises and forgotten tombs

El-Bagawat is one of the oldest Christian cemeteries in the world, with more than 260 painted funerary chapels.

IV

Civilisations across time

Pharaonic, Persian, Greek, Roman and Coptic cultures each left their mark here. More than 4,000 years of history in a single oasis.

Visit them onthe Cairo – Kharga route.

Days 8 to 10 of the expedition: Om el-Dabadib, Labakha, the Temple of Hibis and El-Bagawat.
